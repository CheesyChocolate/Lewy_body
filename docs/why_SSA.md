SAA-positivity is a biomarker for synucleinopathy (Lewy body pathology), not a diagnosis of DLB specifically. What CSF/skin/olfactory alpha-synuclein SAA detects is misfolded alpha-synuclein aggregates - the same pathological substrate shared across the Lewy body spectrum:

- DLB (dementia with Lewy bodies)
- Parkinson's disease (with or without dementia, PD/PDD)
- Isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD) - often SAA-positive years before any motor/cognitive syndrome
- Multiple system atrophy (MSA) - technically also a synucleinopathy, though SAA assays are typically tuned to distinguish MSA's alpha-synuclein conformer from the Lewy body conformer (Parkinson's/DLB-type), with reasonable but imperfect discrimination
- Pure autonomic failure, and some cognitively-unimpaired elderly with incidental Lewy pathology

In ADNI specifically, subjects are enrolled as CN/MCI/Dementia without a DLB label, so an SAA-positive subject with dementia could clinically be DLB, but could also be AD-with-incidental-Lewy-co-pathology (very common - autopsy series show 30-50%+ AD cases have concomitant Lewy pathology), or PDD-spectrum. An SAA-positive MCI or CN subject is not demented at all, so "DLB" doesn't even apply diagnostically yet.

This matches how your own project docs already frame it: docs/data_acquisition.md calls SAA-positivity a "synucleinopathy-positive proxy," not a DLB label, precisely because ADNI can't assign DLB. So the cohort should be understood/described as synucleinopathy-positive (Lewy body pathology present), with DLB being the presumed dominant clinical phenotype in the demented subset given ADNI's inclusion criteria, but not something the label guarantees case-by-case.
